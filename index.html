How we work

Principal, never agent

TERRACE ASSOCIATES LTD was incorporated in England and Wales on 18 July 2012 under company number 08147834, with its registered office at Northway Farm, Charing Hill, Charing in Kent, and it has had the same director for all fourteen years. The registered activity is the buying and selling of our own real estate.

Being a principal rather than an agent is the fact that shapes everything else. We are the buyer or we are the seller, we act for nobody else, we hold no client money, and we charge sellers nothing. It also means the agent protections do not apply, which we say rather than let anyone assume. Two commitments follow. The first concerns material information: since April 2025 the unfair commercial practices rules have sat in the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act, and the trap in property is not the false statement but the omission, because a description that is accurate as far as it goes can still mislead by what it leaves out. So price, tenure, lease terms, council tax band and the physical facts go out as standard, and so do the things that only apply sometimes and change everything when they do, like flood history, restrictive covenants, rights of way, mining or non-standard construction. Where we do not know, we say we do not know. The second concerns price. Buying directly from an owner means paying below open market value, and the difference buys speed and certainty rather than a service. We put it in exactly those terms, our view on price is a buyer's view rather than a valuation, and every seller is told to get their own before accepting anything from us.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Will you pay market value for my house?

No, and anyone in our position telling you otherwise is not being straight with you. Buying directly means paying below open market value, and what you get for the difference is speed and certainty rather than any service we perform. That can be exactly the right trade when a chain has collapsed or a sale has to happen by a date, and it is a poor trade if you have time. You should know which situation you are in before you talk to anyone, including us.

Is your offer a valuation?

No. It is the view of a prospective buyer, which is a different thing from an independent valuation and a different thing again from a survey. It is not prepared by a qualified valuer unless the site says otherwise. Get your own independent valuation and your own solicitor before accepting any offer, including ours. A buyer telling you what your house is worth has an obvious interest in the answer.

What do you have to tell me about a property you are selling?

Everything material to the decision, before an offer rather than during conveyancing. The standard facts, meaning price, tenure, lease terms where it is leasehold, council tax band and the physical attributes. Then whatever applies to that particular property and would change a decision: flood risk and flooding history, restrictive covenants, easements and rights of way, mining or subsidence history, non-standard construction, known defects. Omission is the failure the law catches, not just misstatement.

What if you do not know whether something applies?

Then we say we do not know. The temptation is to leave a silence where an awkward fact might be, and a silence reads as a no to anyone looking at it. If we have not checked something, or cannot establish it, that gets stated as an unknown so you can have your solicitor look into it rather than discovering it later.

Will you charge me anything, or ask for money up front?

No to both, at any stage, including if you decline. We do not ask sellers for upfront payments and we do not charge a fee for making, or for your refusing, an offer. You are free to withdraw at any point before contracts are exchanged. If anyone buying property directly asks you for money in advance, that is the moment to stop and take advice.

Do you reduce the price at the last minute?

Not as a tactic, which is the thing people rightly fear in this trade. An agreed price stands unless a material fact comes to light that was not known when it was agreed, and if that happens you get the reason in writing rather than a phone call the day before exchange. We also do not attach deadlines to offers in order to stop you getting a second opinion. Getting a second opinion is something we actively suggest.
